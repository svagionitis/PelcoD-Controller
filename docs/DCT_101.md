# Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) 101: An Intuitive (ELI5) and Technical Guide

> **Target Audience:** From beginners seeking an intuitive conceptual grasp to software, computer vision, video compression, and optical systems engineers implementing frequency-domain image analysis, autofocus scoring, and digital image transforms.

---

## Executive Summary & ELI5 (Explain Like I'm 5)

The **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)** is one of the foundational mathematical engines of the digital multimedia age. It powers JPEG image compression, MPEG and H.264 video streaming, MP3 audio, and camera autofocus evaluation.

Like the Fourier Transform (FFT), the DCT converts information from the **spatial domain** (pixel brightness) into the **frequency domain** (rates of pattern change). However, unlike the FFT, which uses both sines and cosines with imaginary complex numbers ($j = \sqrt{-1}$), the DCT uses **strictly real numbers and cosine waves**.

---

### The Intuitive Analogy: The Painter's Palette & The Rough Draft

Imagine an artist describing a small $8 \times 8$ pixel patch of a blue sky with a few wispy clouds:

```
[ Spatial Domain: 64 Raw Pixels ] ──► [ DCT Transform ] ──► [ Frequency Domain: 64 Coefficients ]
```

1. **The Spatial Method (Listing Raw Pixels):**
   - You could write down all 64 individual brightness numbers:
     `[142, 143, 141, 140, 142, 144, ...]`
   - This takes 64 bytes of memory, even though almost all the pixels look nearly identical.

2. **The Painter's Method (The DCT Approach):**
   - An artist wouldn't describe 64 separate brushstrokes. Instead, they would say:
     1. *"Paint the whole patch a uniform sky blue."* $\rightarrow$ **The DC Component (Average Brightness)**
     2. *"Add a gentle gradient that gets slightly darker toward the top."* $\rightarrow$ **Low Vertical Frequency**
     3. *"Add a soft horizontal fade."* $\rightarrow$ **Low Horizontal Frequency**
     4. *"Are there any razor-sharp, jagged details or checkerboard noise?"* *"No, zero."* $\rightarrow$ **High Frequencies = 0**
   - With just 3 or 4 numbers, you capture over 95% of the visual information in the block. The remaining 60 numbers are essentially zero and can be thrown away without your eyes noticing.

This property is called **Energy Compaction**.

---

### The Sieve Analogy (Separating Boulders from Sand)

Imagine pouring a bucket of soil through a series of sorting sieves:

```
Raw Visual Data ──► [ Large Sieve: DC & Low Frequencies ] ──► Contains 95% of Image Energy (Boulders)
                              │
                              ▼
                    [ Fine Sieve: High Frequencies ]       ──► Contains Fine Noise / Textures (Fine Sand)
```

- In natural photographs, neighboring pixels are closely correlated. A wall, a face, or the sky does not change color randomly from pixel to pixel.
- The DCT acts like a sieve: it sweeps almost all the visual energy into the **top-left corner** of the block (the "boulders"), leaving the rest of the block nearly empty (the "fine sand").

---

### Why Cosines Instead of Sines? (The Boundary Miracle)

Why do image and video standards (JPEG, MPEG, H.264) use the **Discrete Cosine Transform** instead of the standard Fourier Transform (FFT)?

```
DFT Approach (Periodic Repetition):
[ Block 1 ]  │ [ Virtual Copy 1 ]
   /│        │      /│            <- Harsh artificial cliff edge!
  / │        │     / │               Creates high-frequency artifacts (ringing/halos).
 /  │        │    /  │
/   │________│___/   │________

DCT Approach (Even Symmetric Reflection / Mirroring):
[ Block 1 ]  │ [ Mirrored Copy ]
   /\        │        /\          <- Smooth continuous peak!
  /  \       │       /  \            No cliff edges. High frequencies naturally decay!
 /    \      │      /    \
/      \_____│_____/      \___
```

1. **The FFT assumes periodic repetition:** When you place two blocks side-by-side, the right edge of Block 1 rarely matches the left edge of Block 2. This creates an artificial step cliff, generating fake high-frequency noise that wastes bits.
2. **The DCT assumes mirror reflection (Even Symmetry):** The image is reflected across its boundary like a mirror ($f[-x] = f[x]$). The edges always meet smoothly, eliminating artificial boundary cliffs. Because of this, the DCT packs image energy into fewer coefficients than the Fourier transform.

## 1. What is the DCT? (The 101 Technical Overview)

Invented in 1974 by **Nasir Ahmed**, **T. Natarajan**, and **K. R. Rao**, the DCT was created to approximate the statistically optimal **Karhunen-Loève Transform (KLT)** without requiring expensive data-dependent covariance matrix calculations.

### DCT vs. DFT Comparison

| Feature | Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT / FFT) | Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arithmetic** | Complex numbers ($a + jb$, magnitude & phase) | **Strictly real numbers** (positive and negative amplitudes) |
| **Basis Functions** | Complex exponentials ($e^{-j \omega t} = \cos \omega t - j \sin \omega t$) | Pure cosines ($\cos(\omega t)$) |
| **Boundary Assumption**| Periodic extension ($f[n + N] = f[n]$) | **Even symmetric reflection** ($f[-n-1] = f[n]$) |
| **Energy Compaction** | Moderate (boundary steps scatter energy) | **Superior** (closest fast analytical transform to optimal KLT) |
| **Primary Use Cases** | Audio frequencies, vibration, radar, 2D phase correlation | **Image/video compression (JPEG/H.264)**, optical sharpness scoring |

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

There are several types of DCT (DCT-I through DCT-VIII). The term "DCT" in image processing almost universally refers to **DCT-II** (and its exact mathematical inverse, **DCT-III**, commonly called the **IDCT**).

### 1D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II)

Given a sequence of $N$ real spatial samples $x[0], x[1], \dots, x[N-1]$:

$$X[k] = \alpha[k] \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n] \cos \left( \frac{\pi (2n + 1) k}{2N} \right), \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$$

Where the orthonormal normalization factor $\alpha[k]$ is defined as:

$$\alpha[k] = \begin{cases} \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}}, & k = 0 \quad (\text{DC term}) \\ \sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}, & k \ge 1 \quad (\text{AC terms}) \end{cases}$$

---

### 1D Inverse Discrete Cosine Transform (IDCT-II / DCT-III)

The spatial signal is perfectly reconstructed from its frequency coefficients:

$$x[n] = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \alpha[k] X[k] \cos \left( \frac{\pi (2n + 1) k}{2N} \right), \quad n = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$$

Because the transform is **orthonormal**, energy is strictly conserved (Parseval's theorem):

$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} |x[n]|^2 = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} |X[k]|^2$$

---

### 2D $8 \times 8$ Discrete Cosine Transform

In image processing, images are subdivided into standard $8 \times 8$ pixel blocks ($N = 8$).
For a spatial block $f[x, y]$ ($x, y \in [0, 7]$), the 2D DCT-II coefficient $X[u, v]$ ($u, v \in [0, 7]$) is:

$$X[u, v] = \frac{1}{4} C(u) C(v) \sum_{x=0}^{7} \sum_{y=0}^{7} f[x, y] \cos \left( \frac{(2x + 1) u \pi}{16} \right) \cos \left( \frac{(2y + 1) v \pi}{16} \right)$$

Where:
- $u$: Horizontal spatial frequency index ($0 \dots 7$)
- $v$: Vertical spatial frequency index ($0 \dots 7$)
- $C(u), C(v) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, & u, v = 0 \\ 1.0, & u, v > 0 \end{cases}$

---

### Matrix Formulation and Separability

Because the 2D cosine kernel is **separable**:
$$\cos\left(\frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16}\right) \cos\left(\frac{(2y+1)v\pi}{16}\right) = \cos_x \cdot \cos_y$$

The 2D transform can be computed as two consecutive 1D transforms:
1. Transform each **row** of the block.
2. Transform each **column** of the result.

In matrix algebra:

$$\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{C} \cdot \mathbf{f} \cdot \mathbf{C}^T$$
$$\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{C}^T \cdot \mathbf{X} \cdot \mathbf{C}$$

Where $\mathbf{C}$ is the precomputed $8 \times 8$ orthogonal DCT basis matrix:

$$\mathbf{C} \cdot \mathbf{C}^T = \mathbf{I}$$

```cpp
// Orthogonal 8x8 DCT-II basis matrix C
// C[u][x] = (u == 0 ? 1/sqrt(8) : 0.5 * cos((2x+1)*u*pi/16))
```

This matrix multiplication formulation allows fast, zero-allocation, cache-optimized execution on modern CPUs.

## 3. The $8 \times 8$ DCT Basis Grid & Zig-Zag Scanning

Every $8 \times 8$ block of pixels can be represented as a weighted sum of **64 elementary basis images**:

```
                       Horizontal Frequency (u) ────────►
               u=0     u=1     u=2     u=3     u=4     u=5     u=6     u=7
             ┌───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┐
      v=0    │  DC   │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
             ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
Vertical     │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
Frequency    ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
  (v)        │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
   │         ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
   │         │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
   │         ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
   │         │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
   │         ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
   │         │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
   │         ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
   ▼         │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │
             ├───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼───────┤
      v=7    │       │       │       │       │       │       │       │ Highest
             └───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴───────┘
```

- **Coefficient $(0, 0)$ (Top-Left):** The **DC Component**. Represents the average brightness of the entire $8 \times 8$ block. It has zero frequency (flat wash).
- **Remaining 63 Coefficients:** The **AC Components**.
  - Moving **right** increases horizontal spatial frequency (vertical stripes of increasing fineness).
  - Moving **down** increases vertical spatial frequency (horizontal stripes of increasing fineness).
  - Moving **diagonally** produces cross-hatch textures.
  - **Coefficient $(7, 7)$ (Bottom-Right):** The highest frequency checkerboard pattern.

---

### The Zig-Zag Ordering Path

Because natural images concentrate energy in low frequencies, coefficients are traversed in a **zig-zag order** from lowest frequency to highest frequency:

```
    0  ──►  1      5  ──►  6     14 ──► 15     27 ──► 28
           /      /       /      /      /      /      /
    2     4      7     13     16     26     29     42
    │    /      /      /      /      /      /      /
    3 ──►  8    12     17     25     30     41     43
          /     /      /      /      /      /      /
    9    11    18     24     31     40     44     53
    │   /      /      /      /      /      /      /
   10 ──► 19    23     32     39     45     52     54
         /      /      /      /      /      /      /
   20    22    33     38     46     51     55     60
    │   /      /      /      /      /      /      /
   21 ──► 34    37     47     50     56     59     61
         /      /      /      /      /      /      /
   35 ──► 36    48     49     57     58     62 ──► 63
```

In compression (JPEG), after quantization, the zig-zag scan turns the 2D matrix into a 1D sequence ending in a long run of zeros, which can be stored with extreme efficiency using Run-Length Encoding (RLE).

## 4. Practical Applications & Optical Sharpness Evaluation

Beyond image compression, the 2D DCT is widely used in high-speed computer vision for **autofocus** and **optical blur detection**.

### Why the DCT is Ideal for Autofocus

An optical lens in perfect focus produces crisp, high-contrast edges. When the lens is out of focus (blurred), sharp edges are smoothed out into fuzzy gradients:

```
In-Focus Sharp Edge                           Out-of-Focus Blurred Edge
Brightness                                    Brightness
    ▲                                             ▲
    │         ┌────────                           │             .--------
    │         │                                   │           .´
    │         │  <- Instantaneous step            │         .´   <- Gradual blur
    │         │     (High Frequencies Lit Up!)    │       .´      (High Frequencies Dead!)
────┼─────────┴──────────────► X              ────┼──────┴──────────────► X
```

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    LENS["Camera Optical Lens"] --> SENS["Image Sensor (8x8 Blocks)"]
    SENS --> DCT["2D 8x8 DCT-II"]
    DCT --> SPLIT{"Frequency Split"}
    SPLIT -->|Low Frequencies (u+v < 3)| DC["Average Brightness & General Shapes"]
    SPLIT -->|High Frequencies (u+v >= 3)| AC["High-Frequency AC Sum"]
    AC --> SCORE["Focus Sharpness Score"]
    SCORE --> MOTOR["PTZ Focus Motor Closed-Loop Drive"]
```

1. **Blur behaves as a Low-Pass Filter:** Blurring destroys high spatial frequencies.
2. **Sharpness lights up the High-Frequency AC coefficients:** By summing the energy of coefficients where $u + v \ge \text{threshold}$, the system obtains a real-time, robust **Sharpness Metric**.
3. **Autofocus Peak Search:** As the focus motor moves the lens forward and backward, the DCT sharpness metric traces a bell-shaped curve. The motor simply drives toward the peak of the curve!

---

### Focus Evaluation Metrics Compared

Inside a camera tracking system, several DCT metrics can be calculated on each $8 \times 8$ block:

#### 1. High-Frequency AC Sum (`HighFrequencyAcSum`)
$$\text{Score} = \sum_{u} \sum_{v} |X[u, v]| \quad \text{for } u + v \ge \text{threshold}$$
- Robust, linear response. Excellent general-purpose focus metric.

#### 2. High-Frequency Energy (`HighFrequencyEnergy`)
$$\text{Score} = \sum_{u} \sum_{v} X[u, v]^2 \quad \text{for } u + v \ge \text{threshold}$$
- Strongly emphasizes high-contrast edges over low-contrast texture. Produces a very sharp peak at true focus.

#### 3. Modified DCT (`ModifiedDct`)
Evaluates the directional discrete Laplacian (second derivative) directly in the frequency domain:
$$\Delta H = |2 X[u, v] - X[u-1, v] - X[u+1, v]|$$
$$\Delta V = |2 X[u, v] - X[u, v-1] - X[u, v+1]|$$
$$\text{Score} = \sum (\Delta H + \Delta V)$$
- Measures local micro-curvature; highly resilient to lighting changes and lens vignetting.

## 6. How DCT is Used in This Project (`PelcoD-Controller`)

In the `PelcoD-Controller` codebase, the 2D DCT-II is implemented as a high-performance, deterministic C++17 module dedicated to camera autofocus evaluation, optical sharpness scoring, and lens depth-of-field monitoring:

### 1. Pure C++17 2D $8 \times 8$ DCT Engine ([`Dct.h`](../libs/PelcoDCore/Dct.h) & [`Dct.cpp`](../libs/PelcoDCore/Dct.cpp))
- **`Math::dct8x8(const double input[8][8], double output[8][8])`:**
  - Uses precomputed constant orthogonal basis matrix `kDctBasis[8][8]`.
  - Implements separable row-column matrix multiplication:
    $$\mathbf{T} = \text{input} \cdot \mathbf{C}^T, \quad \text{output} = \mathbf{C} \cdot \mathbf{T}$$
  - Zero heap allocation (`noexcept`, operates completely on the CPU stack).
- **`Math::dct8x8(const uint8_t* block, int stride, double output[8][8])`:**
  - Ingests raw camera image bytes directly from video decoder frame buffers without intermediate copies.
  - Respects pitch byte stride for sub-region memory access.
- **`Math::idct8x8(const double input[8][8], double output[8][8])`:**
  - Exact inverse transform for verifying mathematical orthogonality ($\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{C}^T \cdot \mathbf{X} \cdot \mathbf{C}$).

---

### 2. Optical Sharpness Evaluator ([`DctSharpnessEvaluator.h`](../libs/PelcoDCore/DctSharpnessEvaluator.h))
Evaluates focus scores across live video frames to drive automated Pelco-D lens focus commands (`Near` / `Far`):
- **Region of Interest (ROI) Masking:**
  Focus can be evaluated over the full frame, centered on the boresight target, or restricted to tracking bounding boxes (avoiding blurry backgrounds from skewing the focus metric).
- **Multi-Mode Metric Support:**
  Supports `HighFrequencyAcSum`, `HighFrequencyEnergy`, and `ModifiedDct`.
- **Block Stride Optimization:**
  Allows evaluating every $N$-th block (e.g. `blockStride = 2` or `4`) to achieve sub-millisecond execution times on high-resolution 1080p and 4K video feeds.
